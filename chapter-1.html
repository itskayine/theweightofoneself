Chapter One

The opening of The Weight of Oneself. If it hooks you, the rest is on Kindle.

Arthur woke up today, suddenly, with a weird feeling. Something he somewhat never felt before, a feeling he couldn’t express despite his tremendous vocabulary. “I… Uhh… wouldn’t… Uh… know what has occurred to me”, he thought, dubitative. He, who was most of the time sure of himself–having received many critiques about that–was now discombobulated by this strange awakening. He looked at the time–it was a quarter past 4–. He usually wakes up at 7, so he settled with the idea that this odd feeling was due to his early awakening. Moreover, he spent the last three days sleeping only four hours. This night, he thought he’d sleep more, so he slipped under the warm cover of his bed as soon as he finished his work, circa 8pm.

The German man looked around the room with the greatest care possible, as to not wake the woman sleeping on the bed opposite to his, a woman–claiming to be a duchess from Russia–who was believed to have a mental problem making her delirious. Our Arthur focused on the little aperture under the bedroom’s door. He believed he saw a rather dim light emanating from there. He said to himself that the strange feeling, this bizarre type of nausea, was causing him hallucinations, so naturally, he went back to sleep to try to recover from the deficiency he was suffering from.

As all Germans, this man was an adept of punctuality, he had a really tight schedule which he had been able to manage surprisingly well until now. At 7, he woke up, this time for good. Valeria was still sleeping, he made the smallest and lightest movements he could and successfully escaped the bed, and then the room, without disturbing his sleeping roommate. Without losing any time, Arthur went for breakfast. He was a man of routine. His breakfast consisted of two slices of bread that were covered in peanut butter, a little bowl of oats with exactly 250g of natural yogurt and two thirds of a banana. Every day for the past 3 years, he had eaten these same things. Today, however, he decided he was not that hungry and so, he only took an apple. Breakfast today took him little time compared to the 25 minutes it normally took him to finish eating which, in a certain way, has left him unscheduled free time.

Not knowing what to do, he sat down on the bed, closed his eyes, inhaled and exhaled multiple times. He wasn’t feeling comfortable, he lay down on the floor and repeated the same things. In that position–in which he stayed for the necessary time to carry on with his day–, he imagined that the floor was talking to him. “Stand up! You, my good sire, are hurting me. If you could just move on to the side, that’d be great”. Arthur laughed a little, but he didn’t think much about it. He knew it had something to do with his lack of sleep and that awkwardly weird breakfast he had which he wasn’t supposed to have. He, nonetheless, decided to carry on with his day.

Now came the moment he loved most. He has to get dressed for work. He took off his tiger-stripped pajamas and put on the first layer of clothes for his outfit–an absolutely ravishing white shirt which he inserted into his dark blue pants–. He didn’t forget the straps he held the assembly with and he passed his hands through the sleeves of the slightly darker vest that he had. It was without any doubt that one could say that Mr Arthur was a man of fashion. He dresses simply but very well. He doesn’t dress to impress but he manages to do so. He was one of those men indifferent to the judgment of others. He lived his life as if it was his. He believed in the fact that he was the main and only important character in his story and that this story wouldn’t end without him there to witness it.
